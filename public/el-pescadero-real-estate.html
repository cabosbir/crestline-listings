El Pescadero: Good Food, Open Skies, and Room to Settle In

By Matthew (Matt) Rosenberg, BIR agent and year-round El Pescadero resident.

The first thing that draws you to El Pescadero might be the ocean. The thing that keeps your attention could be dinner. Or the landscape. Or the chance to build a routine that includes a beach walk without making it a once-a-year event.

South of Todos Santos, Pescadero combines an agricultural setting with nearby beaches and places worth going out for. The Los Cabos Tourism Board ↗ highlights nearby Cerritos and San Pedrito beaches. For buyers, the attraction is often the mix of room to settle in and access to the surrounding coast.

Follow the food

Hierbabuena ↗ connects its cooking to seasonal harvests from its organic gardens, with options including the catch of the day and wood-oven pizza. The setting invites you to slow down instead of squeezing a meal between errands.

Agricole ↗ brings together a country shop, kitchen and seasonal experiences. Stop for coffee and bread, explore produce and pantry offerings, or plan around the kitchen’s current schedule. Its strawberries and cream make a persuasive case for leaving room for dessert.

Those ordinary stops are useful during a home search. Find somewhere for breakfast, a lunch spot and a place you would take visiting friends. A property becomes easier to evaluate when you can picture the life around it.

Two beaches, two different days

San Pedrito, also known as El Estero, is a local surf destination with ocean scenery to enjoy from shore. Nearby Cerritos adds surf schools, places to eat and a beach-centered atmosphere. One day might mean watching the waves; another might mean a lesson.

The La Paz Tourism Board’s beach guide ↗ notes San Pedrito’s strong currents and rocky bottom. Check conditions before entering the water and respect access routes. Proximity to the ocean does not mean every property has a convenient route to the beach.

A day that could become your routine

Imagine coffee, a few hours of work, then fresh air before dinner. Another day could mean driving into Todos Santos for galleries and an evening out. The appeal is having choices without feeling obliged to fill every hour.

Test that routine during a visit. Stay for a workday as well as a weekend. Drive from the property entrance to the shops and beach access you expect to use. Notice what feels convenient, what feels peaceful and what would require an adjustment.

Choose a property that makes room for your priorities

Decide what matters most: beach access, room for visitors, a quiet workspace or outdoor living. Then ask how water is supplied and stored, which internet service works at that address, what happens to the approach road after rain and what year-round maintenance involves.

A slower pace can be a welcome change. It still helps to know how that pace fits your needs. Your Baja life can start with a visit; finding the right home starts with understanding how you want to live.

Choose the property that fits

What should you compare in El Pescadero?

Completed houses

Inspect the layout, ventilation, outdoor space, water supply and maintenance needs. Ask how the house performs through different seasons and what the owner spends on regular upkeep.

Land & space to build

Compare usable land, slope, boundaries, legal access and available utilities. Ask a local professional to assess the site and the permissions needed for your intended project before setting a total budget.

Completed condos

Compare the actual unit, HOA budget, building maintenance, parking and any rental rules. Confirm which shared amenities are operating and what your dues cover.

What should a Pescadero property shortlist include?

  • Water you can document: ask how the particular property is supplied, what storage and pumps are installed, who maintains them and what recent use has cost. Separate an existing supply arrangement from a promised connection.
  • Working connections: ask for current electricity information and test the installed internet with the applications you use. A service advertised for the area does not confirm availability at an individual lot.
  • The usable part of a lot: compare surveyed boundaries, slope, access, drainage and the space your proposed building would occupy. Request professional checks of title, permitted use and service arrangements before budgeting construction.
  • The surroundings through the year: ask about nearby activity, dust, road maintenance, rainy-season access and changes between busy and quiet months. Visit at different times of day.
  • Care while you are away: identify who would check the property, water systems and garden, and request a written scope and cost. Include that cost when comparing a house with a condo.

Questions to answer before buying

Is every Pescadero property close to the beach?

No. Confirm the exact location and access route for each property rather than relying on a broad area label. Check the actual drive or walk and any access restrictions.

What should I ask about water, power and internet?

Request details of the current connection or supply arrangement, storage, reliability and typical bills. Verify any promised upgrades independently and test the internet connection if you will work remotely.

How should I compare the price of two lots?

Compare more than price per square meter. Legal access, services, slope, permitted use and site preparation can change the total cost of achieving the same building plan.
