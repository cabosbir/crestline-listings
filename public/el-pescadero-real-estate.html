What makes living in El Pescadero different?

El Pescadero sits on the Pacific side, south of Todos Santos. Its agricultural setting is part of its identity: the Los Cabos Tourism Board describes local cultivation of produce including tomatoes, chiles and basil. Explore the mix of agriculture, nearby beaches and town services during a property visit.

Local background: Los Cabos Tourism Board on Pescadero ↗ and Pescadero’s agricultural setting ↗.

Compare the town, the beach route and your daily routine

When you shortlist properties, mark the exact entrance, your grocery stops and the beach access you expect to use. Compare how often you would drive toward Todos Santos or Cabo San Lucas. Test the final stretch of road yourself rather than applying one advertised travel time to every property in the area.

San Pedrito and Cerritos offer different beach experiences

The La Paz Tourism Board identifies both as local surf destinations and notes San Pedrito’s strong currents and rocky bottom. If surfing is central to your move, choose a break suited to your experience and check current conditions. If your priority is walking by the ocean, assess the access route and beach setting separately.

The same tourism board highlights garden-based dining at Hierbabuena. Exploring the area’s food and farm setting is a useful part of a visit, alongside checking the practical services your household needs.

What should you compare in El Pescadero?

Completed houses

Inspect the layout, ventilation, outdoor space, water supply and maintenance needs. Ask how the house performs through different seasons and what the owner spends on regular upkeep.

Land & space to build

Compare usable land, slope, boundaries, legal access and available utilities. Ask a local professional to assess the site and the permissions needed for your intended project before setting a total budget.

Completed condos

Compare the actual unit, HOA budget, building maintenance, parking and any rental rules. Confirm which shared amenities are operating and what your dues cover.

What should a Pescadero property shortlist include?

  • Water you can document: ask how the particular property is supplied, what storage and pumps are installed, who maintains them and what recent use has cost. Separate an existing supply arrangement from a promised connection.
  • Working connections: ask for current electricity information and test the installed internet with the applications you use. A service advertised for the area does not confirm availability at an individual lot.
  • The usable part of a lot: compare surveyed boundaries, slope, access, drainage and the space your proposed building would occupy. Request professional checks of title, permitted use and service arrangements before budgeting construction.
  • The surroundings through the year: ask about nearby activity, dust, road maintenance, rainy-season access and changes between busy and quiet months. Visit at different times of day.
  • Care while you are away: identify who would check the property, water systems and garden, and request a written scope and cost. Include that cost when comparing a house with a condo.

Questions to answer before buying

Is every Pescadero property close to the beach?

No. Confirm the exact location and access route for each property rather than relying on a broad area label. Check the actual drive or walk and any access restrictions.

What should I ask about water, power and internet?

Request details of the current connection or supply arrangement, storage, reliability and typical bills. Verify any promised upgrades independently and test the internet connection if you will work remotely.

How should I compare the price of two lots?

Compare more than price per square meter. Legal access, services, slope, permitted use and site preparation can change the total cost of achieving the same building plan.
