By Don Weis · Baja International Realty

A personal perspective after more than 25 years

I've known Rolling Hills Estates for more than two decades. I always liked the location, but in the early days it had some real drawbacks. There was no CFE electrical connection, no water connection, and only a couple of paved roads. Twenty-five years ago, it felt a little far out of town. Even ten years ago, you could still say that.

Cabo has grown a lot since then, especially along the Pacific side. Rolling Hills feels much better connected today, while still offering the quiet and space that drew me to it in the first place.

A location that has come into its own

The entrance to Diamante Resort, home to Nobu and Hard Rock, is about 3.5 miles away. Oleada, with Grand Hyatt and the planned new Conrad Hotel, and Rancho San Lucas, home to Grand Solmar, are about 3 miles away. Having major resorts and golf courses less than four miles down the road is a big change from the Cabo I remember. Oleada and Rancho San Lucas currently offer public access, with Diamante generally tougher to arrange. I know the Pacific land in this area as I sold the land that is now Oleada for 51 million dollars 20 years ago.

I expect the Pacific side to keep growing, with new resorts soon to be on both sides of Rolling Hills. There is no stopping it. It is the Pacific side’s turn to boom, and it has already started. One thing I particularly like about Rolling Hills is its elevation, roughly 100 to 200 meters above the nearby resort areas. That high ground gives most properties broad views over the coast and out to the blue Pacific that cannot ever be lost. I know people promise that, but come check out Rolling Hills Estates and the truth will be obvious.

The newer highway also makes getting here easier. The drive from the San José airport is an easy 25 to 30 minutes on the toll road, and San José itself is about 25 minutes away. You can reach Rolling Hills without having to work your way through central Cabo. And the toll road can take you straight into downtown Cabo San Lucas.

Room to enjoy being home

The size of the lots is one of the biggest attractions for me. They range from about a third of an acre to half an acre, larger than you find in many Cabo developments. Arroyos run through the community and create additional separation between properties. You get a sense of space that can be surprisingly hard to find here.

I've never quite understood coming all the way to the tip of Baja and then living packed closely together. Even some of the most expensive resort homes have very little space between them. At Rolling Hills, the larger lots and open areas are a big part of what makes being home feel different.

Most roads are now paved and well maintained. In my experience, the road layout and arroyos also help the community handle heavy rain. That matters in Los Cabos, where a storm can quickly make drainage and access much more important than they seemed on a sunny afternoon. Many developments try to change where the water goes. Rolling Hills worked with what nature already created.

HOA dues of around $100 a month are another attraction. They are considerably lower than the dues in many of the communities buyers compare.

Rolling Hills also has access to the beach and the protected turtle reserve directly in front of the community. That open stretch of coastline adds to the quiet feeling of the place. For me, being able to enjoy that setting is a large part of the appeal. For miles in either direction, no people.

Quiet at home with everyday needs nearby

I also like how easy it is to get out and do everyday things. You leave the gated entrance, get on the highway, and in about ten minutes you can reach a good bakery, an organic market, sushi, Italian food, major grocery stores and plenty of other shopping. As this side of Cabo grows, I expect more options to move closer.

For a change of scenery, Cerritos is about twenty minutes away and Todos Santos about thirty. Both make an easy day trip.

Rolling Hills reminds me of old Cabo when I'm there: quiet, open and away from the busier parts of town. But you still have shopping, services and hospitals in Cabo San Lucas within reach. That balance matters to me.

There are wonderful places farther up the Pacific coast and out on the East Cape. I understand why people love them. Depending on how far out you go, though, getting supplies can become a much bigger part of your week, and medical care can be farther away, especially in an emergency. Rolling Hills offers a way to enjoy that quieter lifestyle while keeping more of what you need nearby.

After knowing the area for so long, that's what stands out to me most. You can have room around you, a wide Pacific view and a peaceful place to come home to, without feeling quite so removed from Cabo.
