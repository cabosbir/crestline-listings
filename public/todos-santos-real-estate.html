Todos Santos: A Little Art, a Long Lunch, and a Different Kind of Baja Life

By Matthew (Matt) Rosenberg, BIR agent and year-round El Pescadero resident.

Todos Santos is a good place to take the scenic route, even on foot. You set out for coffee and end up browsing a gallery. A walk turns into lunch. Before the drive home, you have found places you want to return to.

Historic streets, art, food and the surrounding coast give Todos Santos its own identity. Mexico’s tourism ministry ↗ records its designation as a Pueblo Mágico in 2006. For someone considering a home here, the town itself is part of the attraction.

Take your time in the historic center

Start with a walk instead of a long checklist. The Nuestra Señora del Pilar mission and Manuel Márquez de León theater are landmarks in the municipal tourism directory ↗. Leave room for a small shop, a shaded pause or a conversation.

Use the center as a reference point when comparing houses. If cafés and galleries are important to your daily life, test the actual walk or drive from each address rather than assuming every Todos Santos property offers the same convenience.

Make room for art and a long lunch

Galería de Todos Santos ↗ has represented artists working in different media since 1994. A gallery can be an afternoon stop for a visitor and somewhere to return for new work as a resident.

Jazamango ↗ combines a farm-to-table restaurant with a garden and bakery. For a different setting in town, Oystera at Santa Terra ↗ is an oyster bar and restaurant. Choose places you would enjoy returning to with friends, and check current opening details before visiting.

Keep the coast in the picture

The historic center is not on the beach. Town and coast offer complementary experiences, but your route depends on the property. Punta Lobos is among the area’s recognized attractions; Pescadero and Cerritos give you other options for a coastal day.

Compare the exact location, road surface, parking and journey back to town. If you are considering a coastal neighborhood, the Las Tunas guide can help narrow your search. The municipal directory describes Las Playitas as a place for walks and scenery and advises against swimming there. Check local conditions and access at the beach you intend to use.

Which version of daily life are you looking for?

Some buyers want to be near the central streets and restaurants. Others prefer space, privacy or a coastal setting. Visit the neighborhoods at different hours, test the trip to your regular stops and confirm water, utilities and internet for each property.

A beautiful photo tells you something about a house. Your own routine tells you whether it fits. Explore the property, the neighborhood and the life you want to build around them. Come explore the town—let us help you find your place in its next chapter.

Choose the property that fits

What should you compare in Todos Santos?

Houses near town

Visit at different times to understand nearby activity, parking and access. Inspect the building’s condition and ask what is included in the sale and what maintenance is likely to be needed.

Coastal & outlying houses

Verify access, utilities and the route to everyday services. Compare the outdoor spaces, exposure and view from the specific house, and investigate what may be built nearby.

Land for a future home

Confirm title, boundaries, access and permitted use before committing to a design. Budget for site preparation and services as well as the purchase price, and check the requirements for the intended construction.

How should you compare Todos Santos properties?

  • A walk you would actually make: if town living is the attraction, walk from the property to your usual stops, including after dark. Check the route, crossings, lighting and parking yourself.
  • A house inspection suited to the building: for an older property, ask the inspector about roof condition, moisture, electrical and plumbing systems and past alterations. Check any intended renovation with the appropriate local professionals.
  • The tradeoff behind an ocean view: compare the outlook with the drive to town, road access and services. Ask Don about neighboring land and possible development that could change the view.
  • The cost of your intended use: a full-time residence, a seasonal house and a rental property need different budgets. Request actual maintenance and utility records, and verify any rental restrictions before relying on income estimates.

Questions to answer before buying

Should I compare Todos Santos with Cerritos and El Pescadero?

Yes, if you are still choosing your setting. Compare proximity to town, the beach routes you would use, property types and services. A visit to all three can clarify your priorities.

Are condo and home ownership costs the same?

A condo may include shared maintenance through HOA dues, while a house owner may arrange more services individually. Ask for the current fees, bills and maintenance needs of each property you are comparing.

How do I judge an asking price here?

Request recent sold comparables with similar location, property type, condition and access to services. Broad market averages or a reduction from an old asking price do not establish the value of the individual property.
