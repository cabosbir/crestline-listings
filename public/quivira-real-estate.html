Why are we showing only completed properties?

We start with completed properties so you can inspect the actual residence, compare its condition and surroundings, and see which services and amenities are operating. This reduces exposure to construction delays, unfinished amenities and a developer’s need for future sales or progress payments to finish the project.

A completed label is not a guarantee: confirm the property’s condition, documents, utilities and any HOA obligations. We consider preconstruction selectively, with particular attention to the developer’s record of completing projects and financial capacity to deliver through slower sales or rising costs.

Quivira Los Cabos: Golf, Ocean Views & Life on the Pacific Coast

By Hector Mendoza, BIR real estate advisor · Adapted from Hector’s community guide · October 14, 2026

There’s another side of Cabo San Lucas that many visitors never get to experience: desert meeting the Pacific, golf above coastal cliffs, and residential neighborhoods with room to slow down. Welcome to Quivira.

Why choose Cabo’s Pacific side?

Cabo’s marina, nightlife and Médano Beach are well known. Quivira offers a different setting: open landscapes, sunsets and a more relaxed residential atmosphere, with downtown restaurants, shopping and everyday services within driving reach. For a vacation getaway or a full-time residence, the appeal is having both experiences available.

Golf is part of the attraction

The 18-hole Jack Nicklaus Signature course follows dunes, desert and dramatic Pacific cliffs. Quivira advertises play for eligible property owners and Pueblo Bonito resort guests. Owner access and preferred rates are worth exploring, but they do not mean unlimited or complimentary rounds. See the course details and published ranking and confirm the terms for your property.

A second Nicklaus course was announced. We have not confirmed a current opening date. Compare the golf and amenities operating today when making a purchase decision.

A home with a resort connection

Quivira’s relationship with Pueblo Bonito adds restaurants, wellness experiences and resort amenities to the residential setting. Its Q Life program also brings owners together through outdoor, culinary, fitness and community activities.

Hector highlights owner access arrangements with participating Pueblo Bonito resorts, including Rosé and Los Cabos (Blanco) on Médano Beach. Ask him to confirm the current participating-resort list and privileges for the property you are considering. Resort benefits and neighborhood clubhouse amenities are separate things: individual facilities, guests, reservations, charges and discounts can have different rules.

Find the neighborhood that fits your life

  • Copala: hacienda-inspired condos and houses, with a clubhouse, pools, fitness facilities and recreational amenities.
  • Mavila: a village-style setting with condominiums and townhomes.
  • Coronado: luxury houses in a setting known for golf and Pacific views.
  • Alvar: contemporary residences oriented toward coastal views.
  • Old Lighthouse Club: residential options among coastal bluffs, dunes and desert.

The neighborhood name is a starting point. Compare the actual residence, view, outdoor space, HOA costs and amenities currently available. A condominium with less upkeep and a spacious house for visiting family can offer very different versions of life in the same community.

Picture your own Pacific-side routine

Morning coffee looking toward the ocean, an afternoon of golf or a resort visit, then a Pacific sunset from your terrace: the lifestyle is easy to imagine. Hector and BIR can help you connect that picture with an available property, your budget and the details that matter after you move in.

What should you compare in Quivira?

Compare the actual drive into Cabo and the entrance for the property you are considering. Do not treat all Pacific-side addresses as the same location.

Check the services and amenities included with the property. Golf facilities, resort services and beach access may have separate conditions.

What does the condo really cost to own?

Ask for current HOA dues, the approved budget, reserves and any announced assessments. Confirm parking, storage, pets and rental rules for the actual regime and phase. Compare the building and shared systems as carefully as the unit.

Which unit is right for your use?

Check the floor, stairs or elevator access, terrace orientation and noise at the time of day you expect to be home. Verify which listing photographs show the unit being sold and which show common areas. Tell Don whether this is for full-time living, seasonal use or another purpose.

What should you inspect beyond the living area?

Compare roof and exterior condition, cooling, water storage, electrical systems and drainage. Consider who will maintain the property when you are away. A private pool and garden can add enjoyment as well as ongoing costs.

How does the house fit daily life?

Walk the stairs, check parking and storage, and test the route to everyday shopping. If a house is in a managed community, ask about its rules, dues and any separate club arrangements. Compare the finished property with your actual needs, not simply its bedroom count.

What can you actually build?

Start with a survey, the parcel’s legal status, permitted use and any community design requirements. Ask qualified professionals to assess setbacks, terrain, access and restrictions for the project you intend. An attractive asking price does not answer these questions.

What belongs in the full project budget?

Account for professional work, approvals, utility connections, access improvements and terrain-related construction. Compare the total estimated project and timetable with completed alternatives. Keep the land purchase decision separate from assumptions about future resale value.

Prepare for the next step

Compare suitable properties with a clear budget and intended use, then request the documents and inspections relevant to the purchase.
