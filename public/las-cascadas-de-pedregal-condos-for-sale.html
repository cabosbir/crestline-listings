The listing broker’s perspective

Ideas grounded in
what buyers want.

Don Weis represented the developer from the project’s beginning in 2009 and was involved in every original developer sale. His role included advising on the homes being offered, their positioning and their appeal to buyers.

Drawing on his market knowledge, Don proposed changes to the core floor plans with a target listing price in mind. He also developed optional upgrade ideas that allowed buyers to personalize their homes while maintaining an attractive base price and supporting the developer’s target margins.

From the first buildings to the later phases

How the homes evolved
from building 4 onward.

Phase 1 comprises buildings 1–3 and is typically a quieter part of Las Cascadas. Starting with building 4, Don introduced substantial design changes that distinguish buildings 4–9 from the original phase.

Changes and optional upgrades included larger kitchens and kitchen counters, entrance patios, much larger front decks, fire pits, outdoor kitchens, built-in bars, enclosed garages, storage and hurricane shutters. Two residences also have private pools.

Features vary by condo. Don’s firsthand involvement helps buyers understand the differences between buildings, original upgrades and later owner modifications.

Selling at Cascadas

Thinking about selling your Las Cascadas condo?

Give buyers a reason to choose yours.

Over the years, I’ve learned that selling a condo starts with understanding what makes it different. When buyers see several seemingly identical homes, they may feel little urgency to choose one. A distinctive feature can give them a reason to look closer.

At Las Cascadas, individuality was part of the development from the start. Owners and returning guests know how much the residences differ. I helped propose many of those changes, working with a developer who was willing to make them happen.

Building 3: the first change during Phase 1

While Phase 1 was under construction, Building 3 was planned with three smaller, standard two-bedroom condos on each of floors 1–3. I asked whether the third floor could instead have two larger “Grande 2 Bedroom” condos. The developer agreed.

Making that change required revised supports and a steel I-beam. The resulting space allowed ducting for central air conditioning and a recessed kitchen ceiling—a feature carried through the rest of the project. Having a willing developer was essential to turning those ideas into homes.

Buildings 4 and 5: new ways to use outdoor space

Entrance patios were first introduced in Buildings 4 and 5, along with options for fire pits, outdoor BBQ kitchens, built-in bars, storage units and enclosed garages. These choices gave buyers more ways to make their residences their own.

Buildings 6, 7 and 9: the evolution of the decks

Building 6 originally had standard front decks planned. As the project’s only L-shaped building, it offered an opportunity for angled decks that made better use of its configuration. It also introduced distinctive entrance patio layouts found only in Building 6.

The angled-deck idea continued in Building 7. In Building 9, the building itself was angled to accommodate more angled decks.

Building 9: from extra land to private pools

During construction of the final building, I noticed unused land in front of two condos and asked the developer whether it could become private yards. He agreed. Those spaces became private outdoor retreats with their own pools.

— Don Weis, Baja International Realty

Discuss selling my Cascadas condo

What does the condo really cost to own?

Ask for current HOA dues, the approved budget, reserves and any announced assessments. Confirm parking, storage, pets and rental rules for the actual regime and phase. Compare the building and shared systems as carefully as the unit.

Which unit is right for your use?

Check the floor, stairs or elevator access, terrace orientation and noise at the time of day you expect to be home. Verify which listing photographs show the unit being sold and which show common areas. Tell Don whether this is for full-time living, seasonal use or another purpose.

Prepare for the next step

Compare suitable properties with a clear budget and intended use, then request the documents and inspections relevant to the purchase.
